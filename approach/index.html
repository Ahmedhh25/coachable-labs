Why we work across everything at once

This is the one page where the argument gets made. It is not repeated anywhere else on the site.

Advice has reached you one domain at a time

You are not under-informed. The advice you have had was mostly good, and it arrived from people who each saw one slice of your situation — the career coach who never asked about the marriage, the financial adviser who never asked what the money was for, the friend who only ever sees you on a good day.

Nobody has put your work, the people closest to you, and who you take yourself to be in the same room. That is the gap this is built for: one coach, one view of the whole situation, and a set number of sessions to work it through.

The framework

Where it comes from

Most people offering this have a certification and a scheduling link. What sits underneath ours is a body of research into what makes a society function — not what makes an individual feel better, but what a working society needs from the people in it.

From that research we drew an archetype: a description of the characteristics shared by the people who hold a society together. Steady judgment. Follow-through nobody has to police. A sense of what they are stewarding and who comes after them. Service that costs something. The session arc is built directly around those characteristics, one at a time, in a deliberate order.

The seven sectors

Before the first session you fill in an assessment across seven sectors, and your coach reads it beforehand:

  • Social
  • Career
  • Financial
  • Spiritual
  • Physical
  • Intellectual
  • Family

Nobody is strong across all seven, and the interesting finding is almost never the weakest one on its own. It is which sector is quietly paying for another — the career being funded by the health, the finances being propped up by the family.

Why the order matters

The arc opens on purpose, because every decision after it gets judged against that. Accountability is then set up early rather than saved for the end, which is the part most people find surprising. The reason is simple: a purpose that nobody is holding you to does not survive contact with a full calendar. Putting the accountability in late means watching the first four sessions quietly come apart.

What it rests on

The framework also takes seriously what is known about wellbeing at the income levels most of our clients are at. Killingsworth's 2021 study found that wellbeing keeps climbing with income, and that a person's felt sense of control over their own life tracks closely with it.1 That is the thing the work is actually aimed at: not more, but authorship of it.

Footnote

  1. Killingsworth, M. A. (2021). Experienced well-being rises with income, even above $75,000 per year. PNAS, 118(4). ↩
